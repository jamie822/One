02

The mark

Primary — gradient
Story — four booked, one in, one free
One colour — dark grounds
One colour — light grounds and print

A spiral-bound wall diary, nearly full: five slots filled, one empty. The empty slot is the whole idea — never fill all six. Clear space around the mark is one diary cell. Keep the binding rings except at true favicon size. Wordmark minimum 16px tall; below that, mark alone. Never stretch, recolour off-palette, or add effects.

Short sentences. Full stops over commas. Real numbers and real jobs, never percentages. The kind of writing a spark reading it on a scaffold at 7am would nod at rather than smell marketing in.

Weights 400, 500, 700. 16–18px, line-height 1.6, measure near 65 characters.

Numbers are the punctuation

£99

A price, a turnaround, a count — set it in Clash Display at display size in yellow and let it carry the section. It does more work than a paragraph, and it is the most recognisable thing about the brand's layouts.

Buttons and links

44px minimum on phone, 34px on desktop. Yellow for the thing you want pressed, cyan for things arriving. Never both competing on one screen.

04

Voice

Do

  • Short sentences. Full stops over commas.
  • Real numbers and real jobs, never percentages.
  • Say the price. Trades distrust "get in touch for pricing".
  • "Sorted", "no bother", "rammed" — where they land naturally.
  • Honesty over polish. Three weeks means three weeks.

Never

  • Hyphens or dashes in customer-facing copy.
  • The words "jargon" or "fluff".
  • Exclamation marks in cold email.
  • Fake testimonials or invented numbers. Not once.
  • "Solutions", "leverage", "seamless", "elevate".
  • Stock photos of models in clean hard hats.

The test: would a spark reading this on a scaffold at 7am nod, or smell marketing? If it smells like marketing, rewrite it. The audience is trades who have been sold a bad website before, usually by someone who talked down to them.
