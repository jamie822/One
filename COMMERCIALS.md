# Pricing, Packaging & Sales

Research July 2026 into what UK web designers charge trade businesses, what
electricians already spend on leads, and how to price against both.

Companion to `RESEARCH.md` (the market study) and `MASTER_PROMPT.md` (the build).

## 1. The finding that should change your plan

**The trade niche is already commoditised, and it is cheaper than the general
small-business market.**

A generic UK freelancer sells a 5-page site for £1,200–£2,000. A firm that
markets itself specifically to electricians sells one for £299–£799. There is a
dense cohort of them and most publish prices openly:

| Provider | Advertised | Model |
|---|---|---|
| WSSWeb | from £299 | one-off |
| WebLane | from £449 | one-off |
| Wingnut | from £499 | one-off |
| Sparkwise | from £750, live in 24 hours | one-off |
| Trendjackers | £297 / £797 | one-off |
| InstaWebsite | £19.99/mo | monthly |
| Trader Web Solutions | £24.99/mo, no upfront | monthly |
| SteadyWeb | £45/mo, no setup, live in 7 days | monthly |
| Trades Web Design | from £49/mo | monthly |
| WeGrowTrades | £85/mo | monthly |
| Cariad | £99+VAT/mo | monthly |

*(Advertised headline prices from search results, not verified on the vendors'
own pages. Spot-check five before you build a comparison slide.)*

**So "reasonably priced" at £1–2k is not the cheap end of this niche. It is the
top third of it.** You will not beat £299 on price and should not try.

### What you beat them on

Two things those firms visibly cannot do:

1. **A 24-hour turnaround cannot include a real copy interview.** Market rate
   for website copywriting alone is **£1,200–£2,600 for a 5-page site**, with
   the ProCopywriters 2025 survey putting the average copywriter day rate at
   £480. A £995 site that includes genuinely bespoke copy is *cheaper than the
   copywriting alone costs everywhere else.* Say that out loud in the pitch.
2. **None of them can say "my business partner has been wiring houses for 13
   years."** That is the whole moat. It is also the reason the copy will be
   better, because the person writing it knows what an EICR access dispute with
   a letting agent actually feels like.

## 2. Recommended pricing

**£995 / £1,795 / £3,250 one-off, plus an optional monthly plan. Client owns the
site outright on final payment.**

That last clause is the position nobody else occupies cleanly. The £299 crowd
sells ownership with no ongoing value. The £45/month crowd sells ongoing value
with no ownership. You sell both.

### Tier 1 — "Get Found" · £995
Sole trader, domestic, no website, living off word of mouth.

5 pages · copy from a recorded 45-minute interview · mobile-first with sticky
call button · quote form · domain, hosting, SSL · **Google Business Profile
created and optimised** · schema, Search Console, Analytics · accreditation
badges · 6 photos handled · 1 revision round · handover call.

**~10–12 hours. ~£25 direct cost. ~£88/hr effective.**

### Tier 2 — "Get Work" · £1,795 ← recommend this one
Established one or two vans, some commercial, wants to rank.

Everything above, plus 10–12 pages with a page per service · **4 location
pages** · EV/solar landing page · live Google reviews · gallery · GBP including
walking them through video verification · half-day photography · 2 revisions ·
90 days support.

**~19–23 hours. ~£300 direct cost (photographer). ~£85/hr net.**

### Tier 3 — "Get Ahead" · £3,250
3+ vans, commercial and domestic, wants to look bigger than they are.

Everything above, plus 18–22 pages including 8 location pages and commercial
sector pages · logo refresh and van livery sheet · full photography day ·
landlord EICR lead magnet · **recruitment page** (genuinely valuable, nobody
builds these, and hiring sparks is hard) · review-generation system with QR
cards · Google Ads account built · quarterly calls.

**~30–36 hours. ~£700 direct cost. ~£90/hr effective.**

### Monthly plans

| Plan | Price | Includes | Your time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Care** | £39/mo | Hosting, SSL, domain, backups, updates, monitoring, 30 min edits | ~20 min |
| **Growth** | £199/mo | Care + 1 page/month, GBP posts, review chasing, rank tracking, report | ~3 hrs |
| **Growth+** | £399/mo | Growth + Ads management, 2 content pieces, quarterly strategy | ~6 hrs |

Care is deliberately below the £40–£120 UK market band — price it for near-100%
attach. Growth undercuts the £500–£1,200/month that single-location local SEO
normally costs.

**Target 100% Care attach, 25–30% Growth.** Twenty on Care plus five on Growth
is **£1,775/month recurring.**

### Why hybrid, not pure monthly

Pure monthly has two problems. You front 10–20 hours and recover it over 12–20
months, so a month-4 cancellation costs you money. And the model has been
poisoned: Yell customers report **£300+VAT build plus £65+VAT/month**, or an
itemised **£699 design + £45/mo maintenance + £115/mo sponsored** — roughly
£2,600 in year one, on a 12-month contract with no cancellation. One competitor
claims to have saved a Yell customer over £8,000/year.

When a tradesperson hears "monthly" they hear that. You are selling against a
memory. "You own it, and you can sack us any time and take it with you" is the
antidote.

## 3. The anchor: what they already pay for leads

This is your strongest material, because it is a cost they already resent and
can check on their own bank statement.

### Checkatrade

No published pricing, and the secondhand estimates vary wildly — from
£69.99+VAT/month up to a £199–£399/month outlier. Don't quote a single figure.

**Do quote this one:** in April 2025 Checkatrade reset pricing and large numbers
of members reported going from **~£870/year to ~£1,500/year** with no increase
in lead volume or quality. Some plans now add **£5–£40 per lead on top**.

That increase alone — £630/year — is most of a Tier 1 website.

### Per-lead platforms

- **Rated People**: £5–£10 for an EICR, £20–£30+ for a rewire or EV charger
- **MyBuilder**: £15–£40 per shortlist
- **Bark**: credits ~£1.20+VAT; a rewire lead runs 15–20 credits (£22–£40+).
  Since November 2025 credits **expire after 3 months**, down from 12.
  Typically 3–5 trades contact the same customer, so cost per *won* job is a
  multiple of the lead price.

## 4. Three frames that land

**The rent-versus-own frame.** The strongest one, and it fits your model exactly.

> "You're paying Checkatrade somewhere north of a hundred a month. Over three
> years that's four grand plus, and the day you stop paying you disappear. Every
> lead you ever bought went to a page Checkatrade owns, with four competitors
> listed underneath you. The website is £995 once. You own it. It's still yours
> in ten years."

**The one-job frame.** Use their real numbers:

| Job | Typical value |
|---|---|
| EICR | £150–£300 |
| Consumer unit | £780–£1,000 (avg ~£890) |
| EV charger | £950–£1,400 (avg ~£1,175) |
| Rewire | £1,500–£10,000 |
| Day rate | £250–£350 domestic, £300–£500 commercial |

> "One EV charger install pays for the whole website. One rewire pays for the
> site and two years of hosting. If it brings you one extra job a year it's
> washed its face — and if it doesn't bring you one extra job a year, I've built
> you the wrong website."

**The Saturday frame**, for "I'll just do it myself on Wix":

> "Would you rather spend three Saturdays in a website editor, or three
> Saturdays on the tools at £300 a day?"

Three Saturdays of foregone work is ~£900, and the result is worse.

### Presentation mechanics

- **Always three tiers, always recommend the middle.** Without the £3,250,
  £1,795 reads as expensive. With it, it reads as sensible.
- **Lead with monthly equivalent, close on the total.** "£1,795, or £150 a month
  for a year — less than your Checkatrade bill. And at the end you stop paying
  and still own it."
- **Never say "website." Say "the thing that fills your diary in February."**
- **Put the electrician on every sales call.** £995 from someone who can talk
  18th Edition amendments is a different price from £995 from a web designer.
- **Don't discount — descope.** Too much at £1,795? Drop two location pages and
  the photography, it's £1,395. Discounting teaches them the first number was
  fiction.
- **Mention VAT if you're not registered.** Against a VAT-registered agency at
  £1,795+VAT, you're 17% cheaper to a non-VAT-registered sole trader.

| Logo + van livery | 3–5 hrs | £250–£1,500 | £450 |

| Ongoing SEO | 3–6 hrs/mo | £400–£1,200/mo | £199–£399/mo |
| Review management | ~30 min/mo | £20–£50/mo tooling; agencies from £297/mo | £49/mo or £295 setup |
| Ads management | 2–4 hrs/mo | 5–10% of spend, or £300–£800 base | £250/mo + 10% over £2k spend |

**Standalone GBP setup at £195 is a good foot-in-the-door** for someone who
won't buy a site yet. It takes an hour and it's what actually drives the map
pack.

**Only sell Ads to Tier 3 clients with real capacity.** Ads generate calls fast,
and a spark who can't answer the phone will churn and blame you.

One caution: mark things up openly. "Hosting is £39/month, that covers the
server, backups, security and your edits" is fine. Undisclosed kickbacks are the
thing you're positioning against.

## 6. Payment terms

### The legal trap in the standard advice

The usual advice is a 50% non-refundable deposit. **Don't.** Under UK law,
deposits described as non-refundable are generally enforceable only up to
roughly 10–25% of total price; a 50% non-refundable deposit risks being struck
down as a penalty clause.

Take a **50% payment on account** instead, with the contract saying that on
cancellation you retain it *to the extent of work performed*, at a stated hourly
rate. Same commercial outcome, far more defensible.

### Structure

| Tier | Schedule |
|---|---|
| £995 | 50% on signature, 50% on approval before go-live |
| £1,795 | 40% deposit, 30% on design approval, 30% before go-live |
| £3,250 | 33 / 33 / 34 on the same milestones |
| Monthly | Direct Debit, first payment on go-live, 30 days' notice |

### Protections

1. **Withhold go-live until final payment.** Show it on a staging URL; the DNS
   switch is the trigger. The single most effective lever at this price point.
2. **IP assigns on payment in full.** Standard UK practice, and doubles as
   leverage.
3. **Register the domain in the client's name** but keep it on your registrar
   account until paid. The ethics of this cost you nothing and separate you from
   Yell.
4. **GoCardless for recurring** — 1% + 20p capped at £4, no monthly fee under
   ~1,000 mandates, and **no chargebacks** because funds are pulled directly.
   Stripe for build fees.
5. **Late payment rights apply automatically** — the Late Payment of Commercial
   Debts (Interest) Act 1998 gives 8% above base rate plus fixed compensation
   (£40 under £1,000, £70 for £1,000–£9,999), even if the invoice doesn't
   mention it. Put it in your terms anyway as a deterrent.
6. **Cap revisions explicitly.** "2 rounds included, further changes £65/hr."
   Scope creep will hurt your margin more than non-payment ever does.
7. **Content deadline with a parking clause.** "If materials aren't supplied
   within 30 days the project is parked and the balance falls due." Chasing
   photos from a spark who's in a loft is the number one cause of projects
   stalling at 80% done and 50% invoiced.
