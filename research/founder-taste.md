# Founder taste profile

The founder's recorded approvals and rejections. Every build reads this
BEFORE design work starts. This file outranks generic craft rules wherever
they conflict: the founder is the client of record for the studio's look.
Append a dated entry after every piece of design feedback; never delete
history.

## Hard rules distilled

1. **Every hero is LIVE and immersive, and the moving thing takes up the
   majority of the hero.** Two animated words do not qualify. The bar is
   the v1 "green" hero and its descendants: ES Elec's spinning core with
   constellation and aurora, Loxley's rising-and-setting sun. This is the
   FLOOR for every client site (confirmed 2026-07-28).
2. **Built scenes beat photographs in heroes.** A full-bleed photo of a
   terrace street was rejected as "something off Coronation Street". Code
   scenes (canvas, SVG, gradients, glow) read premium; stock-feeling or
   soap-opera-feeling photography does not. Photos can support inner
   sections; they do not carry heroes.
3. **Movement must be visible on arrival.** If the OS reduce-motion
   setting or a script-timing bug stills the page, the founder reads it as
   broken. Demo/preview links force motion on; live sites may honour
   reduce-motion (accessibility), but the default experience MOVES.
4. **Light-and-flat reads as "1990s" to the founder.** Loxley's first
   all-white data-forward pass was rejected on sight despite clean craft
   scores. Light directions need depth: big animated focal elements, glow,
   layers, scene-building. Whitespace alone is not premium here.
5. **Drawn celestial/energy focal objects want to look like the real
   thing.** The sun iterations: bigger ("a larger sun rising and
   setting"), then crisp and round with rays, then rays softer, then MORE
   rays around the whole disc and shorter, then fades that MEET so nothing
   reads spiky. Lesson: focal objects get iterated to organic softness —
   start with dense, soft, blended detail, not sparse geometric spikes.
6. **Positive energy.** Earlier (Stacked Out) feedback: "not as dark and
   moody... this is supposed to be a positive thing". Dark directions are
   fine (Tungsten approved) but must feel warm and alive, not gloomy.
7. **Composited elements must integrate.** "Looks stuck on and out of
   place" was the harshest feedback of the session (Stacked Out logo).
   Anything overlaid must share the motion/perspective/lighting of what
   it sits on.
8. **Interactive instruments are loved.** The Loxley estimator survived
   every redesign round; the founder called the features "nice" even while
   rejecting the visuals. Calculators/estimators/configurators are a
   signature to repeat.
9. **The Higgsfield expectation.** The founder expects generated media
   (video especially) in the toolkit and asks why it is absent. When the
   connection is available, hero video loops are the upgrade path; heroes
   are built with a scrim slot ready for them.
10. **Uniqueness matters commercially.** "No two websites the same" is a
    sales promise. The direction ledger (max one shared axis) is policy,
    with the one standing shared axis being the live-hero standard itself.

## How to use this file

Before any new client's direction is chosen: read the hard rules, design
the hero as a BUILT, MOVING scene at floor level or above, unique per the
ledger, then show the founder a temp link for sign-off (they own design
sign-off). Record their verdict here, dated, win or lose.
