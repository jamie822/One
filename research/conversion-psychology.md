# Conversion Psychology for UK Trade Websites
Standing knowledge for the studio. Scope: UK electricians and solar installers. Conversions are phone calls first, forms second. Last researched: 2026-07.

Evidence tags: [STRONG] = primary research or large test datasets. [MODERATE] = credible industry data, single well-run tests, or surveys with self-report caveats. [FOLKLORE] = widely repeated, weakly sourced; use with judgement, never cite to a client as fact.

---

## OPERATING RULES (apply in every build)

1. **CTA colour = maximum contrast against ITS surroundings, drawn from the brand palette's accent.** Hue is nearly irrelevant; isolation is everything. One CTA colour sitewide, used for nothing else. [STRONG]
2. **Spend testing effort on CTA copy and placement before colour.** Copy changes routinely beat colour changes by an order of magnitude. First person converts: "Get my free quote", not "Submit". [MODERATE]
3. **Phone number visible in the header on every page, as a real `tel:` link, styled as a button on mobile.** Calls are the money channel: inbound calls convert 10 to 12x better than web leads for local services. [STRONG]
4. **Mobile: sticky bottom bar, full width, thumb zone. Two segments max: "Call now" (tel:) + "Get a quote" (form/WhatsApp). Appear after ~600px scroll so it never covers the hero, persist thereafter.** Because 65%+ of UK traffic is mobile, calls close best, and sticky CTAs are a consistently positive tested pattern. [MODERATE]
5. **Repeat the primary CTA roughly once per 1.5 to 2 viewport-heights and at the end of every major section.** The end of a section is a natural decision point. Never leave a scroll position with no action reachable. [MODERATE]
6. **The hero must answer in under 5 seconds: who you are, what you do, where you work, and how to act.** Trade + area in the H1, one proof line, one visible CTA. First impressions form in 50ms and they halo everything after. [STRONG]
7. **Design clean and conventional: low visual complexity, high prototypicality.** Users judge sites most beautiful and most trustworthy when they look like what they expect a good electrician's site to look like, executed unusually well. Weird layouts cost trust. [STRONG]
8. **Never trade speed for decoration. Budget: LCP under 2.5s on 4G, ideally under 1.5s.** 53% of mobile visits abandon after 3s; 0.1s of speed moved conversions 8 to 10% in Google/Deloitte's 37-brand study. Never animate the LCP element. [STRONG]
9. **Trust row order for UK electricians: Google review stars + count, NICEIC or NAPIT badge, "fully insured" with the £ amount, years trading, guarantee.** For solar add MCS + HIES/RECC and finance protection. Reviews outrank badges; badges outrank claims. [MODERATE]
10. **Show the real person, the real van, real jobs.** Users ignore stock photos and scrutinise photos of real people and real work. A named face with a photo is the cheapest credibility win available. [STRONG]
11. **Reviews: embed real Google reviews with name, area, job type and date. Target 4.5 to 4.9 displayed, never a suspicious flat 5.0 wall.** Purchase likelihood peaks at 4.2 to 4.7 stars, not 5.0. [STRONG]
12. **DMCC Act 2025: fake, incentivised-but-undisclosed, or cherry-picked review presentation is illegal in the UK. Fines up to 10% of global turnover.** Never fabricate, never let a client supply "testimonials" without provenance, never suppress genuine negatives on a feed you control. [STRONG legal fact]
13. **Domestic electrical: show at least from-prices or a price framework (callout fee, hourly rate, fixed-price jobs like fuse board swaps and EV chargers).** Buyers strongly prefer contractors who show pricing; transparency filters better leads. [MODERATE]
14. **Solar: anchor with a realistic typical-system price range and payback framing, then push to survey/quote.** High-ticket considered purchases need an anchor to stop sticker-shock bounce; the first number sets the frame. [MODERATE, anchoring itself STRONG]
15. **Forms: 3 to 5 fields max (name, phone, postcode or area, what you need). Every removed field pays.** Fewer fields is one of the most replicated findings in form UX. [STRONG]
16. **Frame the form as a callback request with a stated response time ("We'll call you back within 1 hour, Mon to Sat"), and make the client actually do it.** Leads contacted within 5 minutes are ~21x more likely to qualify than at 30 minutes. Speed of response is a conversion feature you can design into the promise. [STRONG for response-time effect]
17. **Offer WhatsApp alongside phone, not instead.** Under half of UK consumers now prefer messaging businesses and 42% have used WhatsApp for it; the 9pm emergency still calls. wa.me link with a prefilled message. [MODERATE]
18. **Build two paths from the hero: emergency path (call now, response promise, 24/7 if true) and considered path (guides, pricing, reviews, quote form).** One site, two intents; never make the 9pm visitor scroll past solar payback charts to find the phone number. [MODERATE, synthesis]
19. **Motion is seasoning: subtle scroll reveals on non-critical content only, 150 to 400ms, once, respecting prefers-reduced-motion. No scrolljacking, no animated headlines, no parallax heroes on mobile.** Motion is processed pre-attentively and steals attention from the content that converts. [STRONG for the distraction mechanism]
20. **Key page copy must survive the layer-cake scan: front-loaded headings that carry the argument on their own.** People scan headings and first words; F-pattern wasteland reading happens only when formatting fails. [STRONG]
21. **Left-align key content and CTAs on desktop; users spend ~80% of viewing time on the left half.** On mobile, centre is fine; thumb zone beats eye zone for tap targets. [MODERATE]
22. **Premium perception = typography discipline + whitespace + real photography + zero template smells.** 46% of credibility comments in the Stanford study were about visual design. Serif or well-chosen distinctive type for headings, one accent colour, no clip-art, no fake urgency. [STRONG for design-drives-credibility]
23. **State the guarantee and insurance explicitly, near the CTA.** Risk reversal near the point of action; unverified specifics ("£5m public liability", "NICEIC Platinum Promise backed") beat vague "fully insured". Only claims the client can prove. [MODERATE]
24. **Area familiarity converts: name the town in headline, show a service-area map or list, reference local job examples.** Local intent is immediate: 76% of near-me searchers act within 24 hours. [MODERATE, near-me stat STRONG]
25. **Never fake scarcity, urgency or reviews. No fear-selling.** Illegal under DMCC/CPRs and it reads as cheap, which kills the premium positioning that justifies the client's prices. [STRONG]

## 1. CTA colour psychology

**What the evidence actually says.** The famous HubSpot/Performable test (red beat green by 21%, ~2,000 visits) is the most misquoted result in CRO. The page was green-heavy; red was the only red element. It is a contrast result, not a hue result. HubSpot's own write-up and later analyses agree. Sources: https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/20566/the-button-color-a-b-test-red-beats-green.aspx (original test); https://cxl.com/blog/which-color-converts-the-best/ (CXL review of button-colour evidence: contrast and visual hierarchy, not hue, drive clicks).

**Mechanism.** Von Restorff / isolation effect: the item that differs from its context is remembered and noticed. Robust lab psychology since 1933. Applied meaning: the button must be the most visually isolated element in its viewport. CXL's survey of 90 high-converting landing pages found orange, blue, red, green all represented; the common factor was strong contrast with the page and white button text (86.6% of them).

**How much colour matters vs copy/placement.** Colour changes typically move clicks single-digit percent when contrast is already adequate. Copy changes move more: Unbounce/ContentVerve tests found "Start my free trial" beat "Start your free trial" by 90% clicks, and Copyblogger's "End My Scheduling Hassles" beat a bigger generic button by 24%. Source: https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/design-call-to-action-buttons/ (Aagaard's first-person copy tests). Placement and repetition (section 2) move more still. Verdict: red-vs-green as hue psychology is [FOLKLORE]; contrast/isolation is [STRONG]; copy > colour is [MODERATE].

**Practical rule for any brand palette.** Pick the accent hue most chromatically distant from the site's dominant background and section colours (complement or near-complement), at a saturation step above everything else, meeting at least 4.5:1 text contrast and ideally 3:1+ against adjacent surfaces. Reserve it exclusively for conversion actions. If the brand is navy, CTA is amber/orange. If the brand is green, CTA is warm orange/red. If the brand is orange, CTA is deep blue. Test copy before ever testing hue.

## 2. CTA count, placement, repetition, click-to-call

**Phone is the conversion.** BIA/Kelsey: 25 to 40% of inbound calls to local businesses become customers, 10 to 12x web-lead conversion; 61% of SMBs rate inbound calls their best lead type. Source: https://www.bia.com/press-releases/phone-calls-are-the-new-click-new-report-by-biakelsey-focuses-on-mobile-local-lead-attribution-for-smbs/ (BIA/Kelsey local lead attribution research). Google/Ipsos (3,000 mobile searchers): 70% of mobile searchers call a business directly from search; near half would be frustrated and go elsewhere if no call option. Source: https://adwords.googleblog.com/2013/09/new-research-shows-that-70-of-mobile.html (Google's own study; dated 2013 but directionally intact). [STRONG]

**Above the fold.** One primary CTA visible without scrolling, plus the header phone number. Do not crowd the hero with competing actions; one primary + one secondary (the two-path split, section 8) is the ceiling.

**Repetition down the page.** GoodUI's aggregated A/B tests support repeating the CTA on long pages; one documented test went from 7% to 12.8% conversion by adding bottom-of-page CTAs. Sources: https://goodui.org/patterns/41/ (Sticky Call To Action, a repeatedly tested positive pattern in GoodUI's 634-test database); https://metrifi.com/blog/research-shows-if-you-repeat-your-call-to-action-more-people-will-convert/ (secondary review of repeat-CTA tests). Cadence rule: a reachable CTA every 1.5 to 2 viewports and after every persuasion block (reviews, pricing, guarantee). End-of-page CTA is mandatory; readers who reach the bottom are the hottest. [MODERATE]

**Sticky mobile call bar.** Vendor claims of "200% uplift" are unsourced [FOLKLORE], but the pattern logic is sound and GoodUI's sticky-CTA tests trend positive: zero-effort access at the decision moment, thumb zone, no hunting. Spec: bottom-fixed, 56 to 64px tall, safe-area padding, "Call [name]" with phone icon as tel: link, secondary "Quote" segment; slide in after ~600px scroll (past hero, intent shown); never overlap form fields or cookie UI; hide when keyboard open. Desktop equivalent: sticky header with phone number top right. [MODERATE]

**Phone vs form vs WhatsApp for UK trades.** Offer all three, ranked: tel: first (emergency + older demographic + highest close rate), short callback form second (after-hours capture, considered jobs), WhatsApp third (photo-of-fuse-board use case is genuinely valuable for quoting; 42% of UK consumers have messaged a business on WhatsApp, 43% prefer it for contacting businesses). Sources: https://www.digit.fyi/whatsapp-preferred-customer-service-third-of-brits/ (UK survey on WhatsApp preference); https://sqmagazine.co.uk/whatsapp-statistics/ (UK WhatsApp business usage stats). Track calls (unique number or GA tel: click events) or the site's value is invisible to the client. [MODERATE]

## 3. Trust psychology for UK trades

Ranked by evidence weight for UK homeowners:

1. **Google reviews (stars, count, recency, content).** BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey (annual, ~1,000+ UK/US consumers) is the primary source: minimum acceptable average is ~4.0; consumers read ~10 reviews over ~13 minutes; 73%+ weight recent reviews heavily; willingness to trust businesses with under 50 reviews rose in 2025. Source: https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/ (the standard annual local-review behaviour survey). [STRONG]
2. **Accreditation badges: NICEIC / NAPIT (electrical), MCS + HIES/RECC (solar), TrustMark.** NICEIC is the most recognised electrical body among UK homeowners; a NICEIC/YouGov survey found 30% of consumers attempt DIY electrical work knowing it is unsafe, which is the fear the badge resolves. The often-quoted "9 in 10 feel more confident with NICEIC" is weakly sourced [FOLKLORE tier]. Badge value is real but secondary to reviews; badges must link to the register entry so they are verifiable. Sources: https://niceic.com/ (register + Platinum Promise guarantee); https://www.homebuilding.co.uk/home-improvements/nearly-a-quarter-of-homeowners-at-risk-of-electrical-problems-warns-industry-survey (NICEIC-commissioned survey coverage). For solar, MCS is a legal-adjacent requirement for SEG export payments, so it is a qualification gate, not just a badge. [MODERATE]
3. **Directory endorsements: Checkatrade, Which? Trusted Traders, TrustATrader.** Which? Trusted Trader carries consumer-brand halo (60+ years of Which?); Checkatrade has mass recognition and a 12-check vetting story. Industry surveys claim 87% of homeowners fear hiring trades due to trust concerns, which these schemes exist to resolve. Sources: https://trustedtraders.which.co.uk/why-use-which-trusted-traders/ (scheme description); https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/expert-advice/checkatrade-how-we-work/ (vetting process). Show these badges if held; do not send traffic to the directory (link to profile in footer only, or not at all). [MODERATE]
4. **Real person, real van, real jobs.** NN/g eyetracking: users ignore decorative stock imagery and scrutinise photos of real people and real work as content. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/ (NN/g eyetracking on photo types). Named owner with face photo, van with livery outside a recognisable local street, before/after boards with job description and town. [STRONG mechanism]
5. **Insurance + guarantees.** "£2m/£5m public liability insurance", workmanship guarantee in years, NICEIC Platinum Promise (work covered if the firm folds). No direct A/B data for trades [MODERATE by mechanism: risk reversal]. State specifics, place near CTAs and forms.
6. **Response-time promises and area familiarity.** "Answered within 3 rings", "In [town] daily", "Aiming to attend emergencies within 60 minutes". Backed by the speed-to-lead evidence (section 7) and near-me immediacy (section 8). Only promise what the client will honour. [MODERATE]

Anti-signals that destroy trust: stock hard-hat models, 0800-style anonymity with no address, walls of identical 5.0 reviews, badge soup of 12 unexplained logos, "cheapest in town" framing.

## 4. Pricing display psychology

**Transparency beats hiding, especially for domestic electrical.** Clear Seas Research / ACHR News (US HVAC survey): 71% of homeowners are more likely to call contractors who show pricing online; only ~20% of contractors do. Contractors report online-priced leads close better after the visit. Source: https://www.achrnews.com/articles/153642-contractors-reap-the-benefits-of-transparent-online-pricing (trade-press coverage of the survey). US HVAC, so treat as directional for UK trades. [MODERATE]

**How to show it.** Domestic electrical (urgent, low-ticket): publish the price framework rather than a full menu: callout/first-hour fee, standard hourly rate, fixed prices for commodity jobs (consumer unit replacement from £X, EV charger supply+fit from £X, EICR from £X). "From" pricing sets an anchor and pre-qualifies without cornering the client. Emergency work: state the callout fee plainly; the 9pm visitor fears being ripped off more than the price itself.

**Anchoring.** Kahneman & Tversky 1974 and decades of replication: the first number seen disproportionately drags the final judgement, even when arbitrary. Source: https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/the-anchoring-effect/ (Harvard PON summary of anchoring research). Applied: on solar pages, lead with the honest typical range ("Most 4kW home systems: £6,000 to £8,000 installed") before the quote CTA. This anchors realistically, filters fantasy leads, and makes the eventual quote feel consistent rather than shocking. Good-better-best package framing (3 tiers, middle emphasised) exploits extremeness aversion. [STRONG lab basis, MODERATE applied]

**Solar vs electrical difference.** Solar is high-ticket and considered (research phase runs days to six months, buyers gather 3+ quotes; source: https://www.imperioleads.com/blogs/imperioleads-blog/top-questions-uk-homeowners-ask-before-becoming-solar-leads covering the UK solar enquiry journey). Solar pages must do maths in public: payback years, SEG export, battery add-on economics. Electrical is urgent and low-ticket: price certainty and speed dominate; "free quote" alone is fine for rewires but weak for commodity jobs where competitors show numbers. "Free quote" with zero price context is the lowest-performing default, kept only where genuine job variability (full rewires, EICR remedials) makes numbers dishonest.

## 5. First impressions

**Speed of judgement.** Lindgaard et al. 2006: visual appeal judgements form in 50ms and correlate ~0.9 with judgements at longer exposures; they halo subsequent credibility and usability perception. Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01449290500330448 (Behaviour & IT, the "50 milliseconds" paper). Google's follow-up (Tuch, Presslaber, Bargas-Avila 2012): effects appear at 17ms; low visual complexity + high prototypicality produce the best first impressions. Source: https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/38315.pdf (Google/Basel study, IJHCS). Practical meaning: a trade site should look immediately like an excellent version of the expected thing, not a novel thing. [STRONG]

**Hero 5-second test.** Within one glance the page must answer: What trade? What area? Can I trust you? What do I do next? Concretely: H1 = service + area ("Electricians in Ross-on-Wye & Hereford"), subline = proof (NICEIC, review score, years), one primary CTA + phone, one real photo. Validate with a 5-second test (Lyssna/UsabilityHub) on every hero before shipping. [MODERATE method, strong face validity]

**Scanning patterns.** NN/g eyetracking: F-pattern is what users do when formatting gives them no structure; the efficient layer-cake pattern (headings + subheadings) is what good formatting produces. ~80% of viewing time is on the left half. On mobile the pattern compresses; users fixate centrally and thumb-scroll, so vertical rhythm and heading quality matter more than left/right position. Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/ (F-pattern revisited, incl. mobile); https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/ (layer-cake pattern). Applied: headings must carry the sales argument alone; front-load first 2 words of every heading and bullet. [STRONG]

## 6. Motion, interactivity, performance

**When motion pays.** Feedback (button press states, form validation), orientation (accordion expand), and drawing attention to ONE thing at the right moment. Motion is pre-attentive: eyes go to it involuntarily, which is precisely why it must be rationed near conversion elements. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-usability/ (NN/g, Animation for Attention and Comprehension). [STRONG]

**When motion costs.** NN/g on scroll-triggered text animation: delays reading, adds cognitive load, and frustrates when repeated; scrolljacking plus fades produce overload. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-animations/ (NN/g eyetracking-informed critique). Count-ups and parallax have no conversion evidence in their favour; treat as brand seasoning only, never on the emergency path. Rules: reveal once, never re-trigger; 150 to 400ms; opacity/transform only; nothing above the fold moves on load; respect prefers-reduced-motion; the phone number never animates. [STRONG for costs, FOLKLORE for "engagement lift" claims]

**Performance is the highest-leverage "motion" decision.** Google/SOASTA: 53% of mobile visits abandoned over 3s; bounce probability +32% from 1s to 3s. Source: https://business.google.com/ca-en/think/marketing-strategies/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks/ (Google mobile speed benchmarks). Google/Deloitte "Milliseconds Make Millions" (37 European brands, real RUM data): 0.1s faster mobile load = +8.4% retail conversions, +10.1% travel. Source: https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9757/Milliseconds_Make_Millions_report_hQYAbZJ.pdf (the primary report). A GSAP bundle that adds 400ms to LCP must buy more than 30% relative bounce cost. It never does on a trade site. Native CSS first. [STRONG]

## 7. Form psychology

**Field count.** Baymard (e-commerce checkouts, 15+ years of testing): average checkout has 11.3 fields, best practice is ~8, and fewer considered fields reliably reduces abandonment. Source: https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields (Baymard on minimising fields). For a trade lead form the equivalent floor is 3 to 5: name, phone, postcode/town, job description (textarea optional), photo upload optional for quoting. Email optional; phone is the field that matters. Each extra field must justify itself against lead quality, not curiosity. [STRONG]

**Multi-step vs single.** For 3 to 5 fields, single step wins: no evidence supports splitting a tiny form. Multi-step earns its keep at 6+ questions or where qualification helps (solar: roof type, bill size, timeframe), because perceived effort per screen drops and sunk-cost/consistency kicks in after step one. Venture Harbour documented 0.96% to 8.1% on a long enquiry form; treat headline multiples like "86% better" as vendor-inflected [FOLKLORE tier numbers, MODERATE direction]. Source: https://www.zuko.io/blog/single-page-or-multi-step-form (Zuko's balanced analysis from form-analytics data). Start multi-step with the easiest, least personal question (postcode); ask contact details last.

**The callback-request pattern.** Reframe the form from "contact us" (visitor does work, waits) to "request a callback" (business does work, fast). Pair with a concrete promise ("We call back within 1 hour, 8am to 6pm"). Basis: MIT/InsideSales Lead Response Management study: contacting within 5 minutes vs 30 makes qualifying ~21x more likely; HBR 2011 audit found average firm response is 42 hours, so the promise is a genuine differentiator. Sources: https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads (HBR lead response audit); https://www.leadresponsemanagement.org/lrm_study (original Oldroyd study). Design obligation: confirm on screen what happens next and when. [STRONG]

## 8. Local-intent behaviour: the two-path site

**The 9pm emergency visitor.** Near-me/local intent is immediate: 76% of smartphone near-me searchers visit a business within 24 hours (Think with Google consumer insights; source: https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/consumer-trends/near-me-searches-local-business/ ). They are triaging, not browsing: Does this firm cover my area? Are they open now? Will they answer? Needs: tel: link in first viewport, "24/7" or honest hours, callout fee, response promise, area confirmation. They will not read reviews for 13 minutes; a 4.8 (214) summary line suffices. [STRONG intent data]

**The 3-month solar researcher.** Multiple visits, multiple quotes, spreadsheet mindset. Needs: pricing ranges and payback maths (section 4), MCS/HIES proof, component brands and warranties, local case studies with numbers ("3.2kW, Ledbury, £142 first-quarter export"), owner's face, guides that answer suitability/cost/planning questions. Their conversion is a survey booking, not a call-now. [MODERATE, journey research]

**One site, two paths.** Hero carries both: primary CTA = call (emergency/urgent), secondary = "Get a solar quote" or "Book a survey" (considered). Nav separates "Emergency electrician" from "Solar" so paid and local traffic can deep-link into the right intent. Emergency landing pages: minimal, fast, phone-dominant, zero decoration. Considered pages: depth, proof, calculators. Do not average the two into one mushy middle page; that is the most common trade-site failure. [MODERATE, synthesis]

## 9. Social proof presentation

**Effect size.** Spiegel Research Center (Northwestern, purchase data): displaying reviews lifted conversion up to 270% (first 5 reviews carry most of the value); purchase likelihood peaks at 4.2 to 4.7 stars and falls toward 5.0 because perfection reads as fake; negative reviews present = higher credibility. Source: https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/ (the core academic review-conversion study; e-commerce basis, mechanism generalises). [STRONG]

**Presentation rules.** Baked-in curated quotes + a live source link beats either alone: curate 3 to 6 reviews chosen for specificity (job type, town, named problem solved), render them in-page (no third-party JS blocking LCP), each with first name, town, job, month, star row, and link "Read all 214 Google reviews" to the live profile for verification. Specific beats glowing: "Found the fault two others missed, tidy, £30 under quote" outsells "Great service 5 stars". Recency matters: BrightLocal, most consumers discount reviews older than a month; surface newest, refresh quarterly at minimum. Photo+name increases perceived truthfulness of claims (identifiable-source effect; treat as [MODERATE]). Never invent a face for a real quote.

**Fake-looking kills.** Uniform 5.0s, same-day clusters, generic praise, stock avatars: consumers now read these as fraud signals (BrightLocal 2025: rising scepticism, more consumers reading content over stars). [MODERATE]

**DMCC Act 2025 compliance (hard line).** From 6 April 2025 the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act makes it an infringement to submit or commission fake reviews, conceal incentivised reviews, or publish consumer reviews in a misleading way (including cherry-picking positives while suppressing negatives in how you present them); the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover directly, no court needed. Sources: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf (CMA208, the official guidance); https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews (law-firm summary). Studio rules: every testimonial must trace to a real, dated source the client can evidence; label any incentivised review; curation for relevance is fine, but state where the full set lives and link it; never write reviews "on behalf of" happy customers. This protects the client and the studio. [STRONG legal fact]

## 10. Premium perception: colour, type, whitespace, photography

**Design IS the credibility judgement.** Stanford Web Credibility Project (Fogg, 2,684 participants): 46.1% of credibility comments referenced visual design first; information structure 28.5%. "People judge a website by how it looks. That is the first test." Sources: https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/ (Stanford's 10 credibility guidelines); https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/997078.997097 (the underlying study). Self-report caveat, but consistent with the 50ms halo evidence. [STRONG]

**Typography.** Errol Morris/NYT quasi-experiment (~45,000 readers): the same statement was measurably more believed set in Baskerville than in Helvetica, Georgia, Trebuchet or Comic Sans. Source: https://opinionator.blogs.nytimes.com/2012/08/08/hear-all-ye-people-hearken-o-earth/ (the experiment write-up). Do not over-read it (small effect, one passage), but it licenses the studio position: type choice carries authority. For trades: a characterful display face + disciplined neutral body, generous line-height, real hierarchy. System-default-looking type is the #1 "cheap template" smell. [MODERATE]

**What reads premium vs cheap.** Premium: low complexity + prototypical structure (section 5), one restrained palette with a single accent, real photography with consistent grading, whitespace used as confidence (cheap sites fear empty space), specific copy with numbers and place names, fast. Cheap: default trade-blue + yellow bolt clip art, stock handshake/hard-hat photos, badge soup, ALL-CAPS urgency, carousel heroes, dense uniform text with no scan structure. Whitespace-comprehension figures ("+20%") trace to weak old studies [FOLKLORE], but complexity findings and Fogg support the same direction. Photography quality is the fastest single premium lever for a trade client: one half-day shoot (person, van, hands working, completed boards) outperforms any stock library. [MODERATE to STRONG by component]

**Colour psychology tables ("blue = trust") are [FOLKLORE].** Context, contrast, consistency and execution quality carry the effect; hue meanings are weak and culturally noisy. Choose palette for distinctiveness in the local competitive set (audit competitors first; if every rival is navy, premium may be forest green + brass) and for contrast architecture that serves the CTA rule. Aligns with the repo rule: never default trade-blue.

## Cross-cutting evidence hierarchy (for arguments with clients)

Lead with: speed data (Google/Deloitte), review effects (Spiegel, BrightLocal), call conversion (BIA/Kelsey, Google/Ipsos), first-impression research (Lindgaard, Tuch/Google), Fogg credibility, field-count (Baymard), speed-to-lead (MIT/HBR). These survive scrutiny.
Use with judgement: multi-step multiples, sticky-bar uplift claims, WhatsApp preference shares, HVAC pricing survey (US), NICEIC "9 in 10".
Never present as fact: hue psychology, "200% call bar", whitespace percentages, any uncited "studies show".
