# Trade Market Doctrine — UK Electricians & Solar Installers

Operating rules for building sites that convert for UK sparks and solar firms. Every rule below is
grounded in named evidence gathered July 2026. Where a number comes from vendor data or an agency
roundup rather than independent research, it is flagged. Nothing here is invented; genuinely
unverifiable claims were dropped during research.

Research note: direct page fetches were largely blocked by the network gateway in this session;
evidence was gathered via web search with attributed excerpts, cross-checked across multiple
sources where possible.

---

## 1. The phone is the product

- **Design for the call, not the form.** A phone number is the top-performing CTA for 44% of home
  services businesses — ahead of messaging (32%) and online scheduling (31%) (LocalIQ home services
  benchmarks). Typical home-service website forms convert at only 2–3% of visitors (PipelineOn).
- **Click-to-call must be one tap from everywhere.** 60% of smartphone users contact a business
  directly from search results via click-to-call (Google, 2019). ~70–74% of home-services /
  electrical traffic is mobile (Hook Agency; WebTonic electrical landing-page stats). Sticky
  mobile call bar is mandatory, not optional.
- **Answering matters more than the site.** Industry call-tracking data (CallRail/Invoca) puts
  missed calls at roughly 20–30% of inbound volume for home-service firms. Velocify's lead-response
  study (~3.5M leads, vendor data) found contact within one minute lifts conversion ~391%, and the
  widely cited companion stat is that 78% of customers buy from the first responder. Doctrine:
  every site ships with a missed-call fallback (SMS auto-reply or callback promise) and we tell
  clients speed-to-lead is where the money is.
- **After-hours is a real segment.** 25–40% of inbound home-service calls arrive outside 8am–5pm,
  heaviest 5–10pm and Saturday mornings (CallRail-derived industry roundups). State evening/weekend
  availability explicitly; if the client is not 24/7, say what happens out of hours.
- **Forms leak.** Only 40–60% of home-service web-form leads ever become a phone conversation
  (CallRail). Keep forms to 3–4 fields, ask for phone number, set a response-time expectation on
  the confirmation. Embedded booking beats redirect: an on-page booking widget produced ~38% more
  bookings than linking out (WiserNotify test roundup).

## 2. Reviews and proof decide the hire

- **Reviews are read before anyone calls.** 93% of consumers read online reviews before visiting a
  business and 74% check two or more review sites (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey).
  Surface real Google/Checkatrade reviews on the homepage — recent, local, with job type named.
- **Word of mouth is the benchmark to imitate.** In Checkatrade's "State of Trust in Trades" report
  (with Focaldata, 3,000 UK adults): 72% hired a tradesperson in the last 12 months and 69% relied
  primarily on word-of-mouth. The website's job is to simulate a recommendation: named local
  customers, named streets/towns, specific jobs.
- **The trust deficit is the opportunity.** Only 52% of UK consumers say they trust the tradespeople
  they hired (Checkatrade/Focaldata). For solar it is worse: only 13% report high trust in solar
  installation firms (GreenFox Energy sentiment survey) and 61% worry about the trustworthiness of
  solar offers and quotes, rising to 73% among retirees (Energy Saving Trust). Every section should
  be built as an answer to "why should I believe you?"
- **Real photos, never stock.** MarketingExperiments' classic test: a real photo outperformed the
  best stock photo by ~35% on conversion; 83% of consumers say they trust companies more when they
  use genuine photos of team/work (agency roundups). Trade audits repeat it: stock imagery signals
  generic service. Doctrine: no site ships without real van, real team, real jobs.
- **Name the register, show the number.** NICEIC/NAPIT registration (electricians) and MCS
  certification (solar) are checkable public registers — display the actual registration number and
  link to the register entry. WebTonic's electrical landing-page data found a specific credential
  badge outperformed a generic "licensed and insured" line by 14%. Generic phrases like "fully
  qualified" are banned; "NICEIC Approved Contractor no. XXXXX" is the pattern.
- **Guarantees are the strongest single motivator.** In BEIS's UK Rooftop Solar Behavioural Research
  (Basis Social, Research Paper 2021/018), "having a guarantee in place in case anything goes
  wrong" was ranked the top incentive by 40% of considerers; 67% said a guarantee scheme would
  allay repair concerns; 65% said a government-approved installer list would boost confidence.
  Workmanship guarantees and insurance-backed warranties get their own section, not a footnote.

## 3. Pricing transparency is a weapon

- **Homeowners already know the going rates.** Checkatrade/MyBuilder publish cost guides that set
  anchors: standard electrician rates £45–£60/hr (£80–£100 London/South East), day rates roughly
  £220–£400 by region, call-out charges commonly £65–£120, overnight emergency work £200–£350/hr
  (Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Go Assist cost guides, 2026). EICR typically ~£212 average / from ~£250
  domestic; consumer unit replacement £780–£1,000 in 2026 (up ~42% year-on-year on materials).
  A site with zero prices loses to these published anchors.
- **Publish "from" prices for named jobs.** 2026 audits of top electrician sites list transparent
  pricing among the six shared patterns of the best performers (WebCitz/Comrade audits). Fixed or
  "from" pricing on commodity jobs (EICR, fuse board, EV charger, extra sockets) qualifies leads
  and pre-answers the #1 homeowner anxiety.
- **Itemised written quotes are a differentiator, not table stakes.** Only 37% of UK consumers
  received an itemised written quote from their tradesperson (Checkatrade/Focaldata). HomeOwners
  Alliance tells homeowners to demand written, itemised quotes and to get at least three. Saying
  "you'll get a written, itemised quote — always" directly matches consumer-advice language.
- **No hidden fees is now law-adjacent.** The CMA's price transparency guidance under the DMCC Act
  requires all mandatory charges in the upfront price and bans drip pricing. State call-out fees
  and any minimums before the phone call.

## 4. Emergency work is its own funnel

- **Emergency searchers scan, call, and leave.** Emergency-service SEO research (Straight North,
  ALM Corp) describes the behaviour: mostly mobile, after-hours weighted, no browsing — search,
  scan, call. An emergency page has one job: the number, huge, with proof of speed.
- **Promise a response window and honour it.** UK emergency providers advertise 30–90 minute
  responses; one national operator reports 87% of emergency callouts attended within 90 minutes
  (Go Assist). "Same-day service available" style urgency copy lifts emergency-page conversion
  10–20% (WebTonic).
- **Separate the emergency route from the quote route in the header and hero.** ~"a strong
  emergency segment demands click-to-call prominence" — 74% mobile electrical traffic (WebTonic).
  Never make an emergency visitor scroll past a portfolio.

## 5. Solar buyers decide differently: money first, risk second

- **Lead with the bill, not the planet.** Saving money on electricity bills motivates 76% of solar
  "considerers" vs 42% of rejectors (BEIS/Basis Social 2021). Cost is the top barrier: 44% say
  solar is too expensive, 38% say it's not a financial priority (Energy Saving Trust). Headline =
  money outcome; environment is supporting copy.
- **Payback framing is the conversion language.** UK calculators and guides cluster on 6–12 year
  payback (6–9 years at 2026 electricity prices per several calculator sites) with free
  electricity thereafter across a 25-year system life. Frame as "pays for itself by year X, then
  ~20 years of cheap power" with honest assumptions shown. MCS performance estimates follow the
  standardised MIS 3002 methodology — mirror that credibility, never promise yields the MCS
  estimate wouldn't support (RECC flags too-good self-consumption assumptions as a red flag).
- **Calculators qualify and convert.** The strongest solar sites lead with an interactive
  estimate: SunPower's address + bill instant calculator; Heatable's 3D roof-mapping online quote;
  Sunsave's monthly bill-swap framing ("bill £150 → £15/month" case, subscription £106/month).
  Vendor data (ConvertCalculator) claims calculator-generated leads close ~30% vs 5–15% for bought
  leads. Even a simple bill-input estimator beats "contact us for a quote".
- **Bake in the market facts that close deals.** 0% VAT on domestic solar and batteries runs to
  31 March 2027 (worth ~£1,200–£1,600 on a typical system) — a real deadline, use it honestly.
  Smart Export Guarantee pays ~5–15p/kWh for export vs 24–28p import, which is the argument for
  batteries (+£4,000–£8,000 cost, +£300–£450/yr benefit, payback +2–4 years but better lifetime
  ROI, per UK cost guides). These belong on the page, not in a PDF.
- **De-risk explicitly.** 46% of considerers are put off by cowboy-builder stories; 32% simply
  don't trust builders with solar (BEIS/Basis Social). Counter with: MCS number, RECC/HIES
  consumer-code membership (deposit protection, dispute resolution), insurance-backed workmanship
  guarantee, panel/inverter warranty lengths, and named aftercare. Satisfaction proof helps too:
  87% of solar owners are satisfied or very satisfied (Energy Saving Trust) — the regret rate is
  tiny; say so with the source.
- **Expect quote-shoppers.** MCS and consumer bodies tell homeowners to compare multiple MCS
  quotes; HomeOwners Alliance says get three. Make the quote itself the sales asset: itemised
  equipment models, inverter spec, usable battery capacity, and an MCS-methodology generation
  estimate — the exact things RECC tells consumers vague quotes omit.

## 6. Failure patterns that make homeowners bounce

- **The 5-second test.** If a visitor can't tell what you do, where you work, and why you're
  different within ~5–7 seconds, they leave (contractor web audits, consistent across sources).
- **Slow mobile kills.** 53% of mobile visits are abandoned if load exceeds 3 seconds (Google).
  42% leave over poor functionality, 38% over unattractive design (Top Design Firms research, via
  contractor-site audits).
- **The repeat offenders:** phone number buried in the footer; no prices anywhere; stock photos of
  American vans; no service area stated; outdated design read as outdated workmanship; forms with
  ten fields; no evidence of certification. Each is an instant disqualifier against a competitor
  who fixed it.
- **Benchmark honestly.** Average electrical-services site conversion is ~2.8%; optimised dedicated
  landing pages reach ~4.5% (WebTonic). LocalIQ 2025 search-ad benchmarks: home services average
  7.33% ad conversion, electrical 9.08% — but electricians also pay the highest CPC ($12.18) and
  ~$94 search CPL, which is why the organic site converting well matters so much.

## 7. What the best real sites do (studied patterns)

- **Mister Sparky (US, franchise):** guarantee-led hero ("On Time. Done Right." / "We're on time,
  you'll see, or the repair is free"), emergency positioning, punctuality as the differentiator.
- **Mr. Electric (US/UK franchise):** safety and reliability addressed immediately, before services.
- **Expert Electric (Canada):** clean minimal layout, credibility-first, ease of use praised in
  2025–26 audits (Nesta Sites).
- **Heatable (UK solar/heating):** instant online quote via 3D roof mapping, price on screen without
  a salesperson, 10,000+ five-star reviews aggregated and displayed.
- **Sunsave (UK solar):** monthly-cost subscription framing, Which? Trusted Trader listing,
  Trustpilot ~4.6–4.7 surfaced, transparent communication cited in reviews.
- **Cross-audit consensus for 2026 electrician sites** (Comrade, WebCitz, SiteBuilderReport,
  ServiceTitan, Jobber): instant click-to-call, visible service-area map, real photos of real
  work, 30+ local reviews surfaced on the homepage, transparent pricing, Google Business Profile
  integration, certification badges, and a single dominant CTA per page.

## The electrician home page, canonical order

1. **Header:** logo left; tappable phone number top-right with hours note ("Open now — call
   [name]"); sticky call bar on mobile. Emergency link distinct from quote link.
2. **Hero (one screen):** what + where in one line — "NICEIC-registered electricians in [town]";
   subline naming the three biggest jobs; primary CTA = call, secondary = "Get a quote";
   Google/Checkatrade star rating inline; real photo (owner/van/job), not stock.
3. **Trust bar:** NICEIC or NAPIT number, "£X insured", years trading, review score + count,
   guarantee statement. All verifiable, all specific.
4. **Emergency block:** 24/7 or stated hours, response-time promise, call-out fee stated upfront
   (CMA transparency), giant phone number. (Own the after-hours 25–40% of demand.)
5. **Services grid:** 6–8 named jobs (fuse board upgrades, EICRs, rewires, EV chargers, fault
   finding, lighting, extra sockets) each with a "from £X" price and link to its service page.
6. **Real work proof:** photos of actual local jobs with one-line captions (job + area);
   before/after where dramatic (old fuse board → new consumer unit).
7. **Reviews:** 3–6 recent reviews with names, areas, and job types; link to the full Google/
   Checkatrade profile (93% will check anyway — bring it to them).
8. **How we work / pricing promise:** three steps (call or message → free itemised written quote →
   work done, tested, certificate issued). Explicitly: "written itemised quotes, no hidden fees" —
   only 37% of trades do this; claim it.
9. **Service area:** map plus named towns/postcodes (local SEO + instant relevance check).
10. **FAQ:** call-out charges, certificates (EICR/Part P/Building Regs), guarantees, response times.
11. **Final CTA band:** phone number repeated + short form (name, phone, postcode, job).
12. **Footer:** full NAP, registration numbers linked to registers, hours, service towns list.

## The solar installer home page, canonical order

1. **Header:** phone + primary CTA "Get your estimate"; MCS badge visible.
2. **Hero (one screen):** money-outcome headline ("Cut your electricity bill by up to X% — panels
   from £X/month" or bill-swap framing); entry to an instant estimate (postcode/roof or monthly
   bill input); star rating + review count inline; real local install photo.
3. **Trust bar:** MCS certification number, RECC or HIES consumer-code membership, Trustpilot/
   Google score, warranty headline ("10-yr workmanship, 25-yr panels"), years trading.
4. **Savings & payback section:** one honest worked example — system size, real 2026 price, annual
   saving, payback year, 25-year gain — with assumptions shown (MCS-methodology estimate). Note
   0% VAT until 31 March 2027 and SEG export earnings.
5. **Calculator / quote flow:** the interactive estimator in full if not in the hero. Capture
   postcode + bill; return a range, not false precision; offer the itemised survey-based quote as
   the next step.
6. **How it works:** survey → itemised MCS quote (equipment models, inverter, battery capacity,
   generation estimate) → install day(s) → handover, app monitoring, aftercare. Kills the
   "difficult process" barrier BEIS identified.
7. **Real installs / case studies:** local roofs, named towns, actual generation and bill figures
   from customers ("4.3kW in [town]: £X saved in year one").
8. **Guarantees & aftercare:** insurance-backed workmanship guarantee, deposit protection via
   consumer code, panel/inverter/battery warranty terms, what happens if something fails. This is
   the #1 motivator (BEIS: guarantee ranked top by 40% of considerers) — give it real estate.
9. **Reviews:** recent, detailed, install-specific; 87% owner-satisfaction stat (Energy Saving
   Trust) as supporting proof that regret is rare.
10. **Battery & EV-charger extension:** why batteries beat exporting at 5–15p vs importing at
    24–28p; retrofit possible at 0% VAT.
11. **FAQ:** cost, payback, roof suitability/orientation, MCS/SEG/VAT, planning, maintenance,
    what an itemised quote includes.
12. **Final CTA band:** estimate CTA + phone; **footer:** MCS/RECC numbers linked, NAP, hours.
