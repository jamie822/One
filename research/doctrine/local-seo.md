# Local SEO doctrine — UK electricians & solar installers

Operating rules for service-area businesses (SABs): no storefront, van-based, town-radius trade.
Researched July 2026. Primary evidence: Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors 2026 (survey of 47
expert local SEOs, released Nov 2025), Google Business Profile Help & Google Search Essentials,
BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey (2025/2026 editions) and LSA Click Study, Sterling Sky,
CMA guidance on the DMCC Act 2024. Note: direct fetch of some primary pages was proxy-blocked;
figures below were cross-checked across multiple independent write-ups of the same reports.

## 1. Where the clicks are — set the strategy before the tactics

1. Treat the local pack and organic as two separate campaigns. In BrightLocal's LSA Click Study,
   the local pack took ~32% of clicks on local SERPs without Local Services Ads, organic took
   ~50%; when LSAs appear, ~25% of clicks shift to paid. Both channels matter; neither is optional.
2. Weight effort by the Whitespark 2026 factor groups for pack rankings: GBP signals ~32%,
   review signals ~20% (up from 16% in 2023), on-page ~15–19%, links ~15%, with citations a
   single-digit share that has declined for a decade. Spend client-facing time on GBP and reviews;
   spend studio time on the site and links.
3. Know the top three individual pack factors (Whitespark 2026): primary GBP category (score 227),
   proximity to searcher (225), keywords in the GBP business title (223). You cannot move proximity
   and you must not fake the title — so the primary category is the biggest lever you legitimately control.
4. For local organic rankings, on-page content and inbound link quality/authority lead (link
   authority was the top local-organic group in Whitespark's surveys). The website exists to win
   this half and to feed prominence back into the pack.
5. In BrightLocal's click testing, review rating was the single biggest motivator of clicks,
   followed by review count. Ranking without a competitive star rating still loses the click.

## 2. Google Business Profile — the single biggest lever

6. Set up every electrician/solar client as a service-area business: address entered for
   verification, then hidden. Google's guidelines ("Guidelines for representing your business on
   Google") require SABs without signed premises to clear the address and list service areas instead.
7. Keep the declared service area within Google's stated bound: roughly 2 hours' driving time from
   base. List real towns/postcode districts served, not aspirational regions. Sterling Sky's testing
   shows the declared service area does not itself drive rankings — it's display, not a ranking hack.
8. Use the exact real-world business name. "Smith Electrical" not "Smith Electrical – Emergency
   Electrician Manchester". Google's policy forbids added descriptors; documented outcomes include
   warnings, feature loss, and soft suspension (Sterling Sky's 50-case keyword-stuffing study).
   Never advise a client to keyword-stuff the name, even though it demonstrably still ranks.
9. Primary category: "Electrician" for electricians; "Solar energy company" or "Solar energy
   contractor" (whichever is available and closest) for solar-led firms — not "Electrician", which
   dilutes solar relevance. Add secondaries for real adjacent work only (e.g. Electrician,
   Electric vehicle charging station contractor, Solar energy equipment supplier).
10. Complete everything Google scores as relevance: services (with descriptions), attributes, hours,
    description, opening date. Google's official ranking guidance ("Tips to improve your local
    ranking") is relevance + distance + prominence, and states review count and review score
    factor into ranking, as do links and completeness.
11. Prepare the client for video verification — the default for SABs now. One continuous, unedited,
    30+ second live recording showing (a) street signage/landmarks proving the service area and
    (b) branded van, tools, and work materials proving the business. Review takes ~5 business days.
    No sensitive documents on camera.
12. Keep the profile visibly alive: photos of real jobs monthly, posts, Q&A seeded with genuine
    FAQs, services updated. Whitespark 2026's headline trend is behavioral/engagement signals
    rising — profiles that "look alive" outrank abandoned ones.
13. Point the GBP website field at the homepage for single-area firms. Tag it with UTM parameters
    (e.g. utm_source=GBP) so GBP traffic is measurable in analytics — otherwise it reports as direct/organic.

## 3. Reviews — acquisition, response, and the law

14. Make Google reviews the priority platform: 83% of consumers use Google to read local reviews
    (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025).
15. Build review velocity, not a one-off blitz. Recency now beats volume: 32% of consumers look
    for reviews from the last two weeks (up from 20% year-on-year) and ~74% want reviews from the
    last three months (BrightLocal LCRS 2026). A steady 2–4 reviews/month beats 40 reviews at launch.
16. Ask every satisfied customer, every time — asking is explicitly allowed by Google. Use a QR
    card in the van, a post-job SMS/email with the direct review link. No incentives of any kind.
17. Never gate. Google's policy: don't "discourage or prohibit negative reviews or selectively
    solicit positive reviews." Sentiment-screening funnels (happy → Google, unhappy → private form)
    risk removal of ALL the profile's reviews.
18. DMCC Act 2024 (in force 6 April 2025) is a hard legal constraint, not an SEO nicety: fake
    reviews, commissioned reviews, and concealed incentivised reviews are banned. CMA can fine up
    to the higher of 10% of global turnover or £300,000 (individuals up to £150,000), and began an
    active compliance sweep in July 2025. Incentive = cash, discount, freebie, or any financial
    interest. Put this in the client onboarding pack in writing.
19. If a client site publishes reviews/testimonials, the DMCC imposes a publisher duty: take
    "reasonable and proportionate steps" to prevent and remove fake or concealed-incentive reviews.
    Only publish testimonials that are real, attributable, and dated; never invent them for copy.
20. Respond to every review, positive and negative: 89% of consumers expect responses, and 63%
    expect one within a week (BrightLocal). Set the client up with a response habit or handle it
    as a service. Responses to negatives are read by future customers, not the reviewer.
21. Spread secondary reviews where the client already has a presence (Checkatrade, Trustpilot,
    Which? Trusted Traders) — Google's prominence signal draws on reputation across the web, and
    those pages rank for the client's brand searches.

## 4. Citations & NAP — foundational, finite, then stop

22. Citations are table stakes, not a growth channel: Whitespark's own surveys show citation
    signals declining in importance for a decade (single-digit % of pack weight by 2021). Do a
    fixed foundational set once, ensure accuracy, then reinvest time in reviews and links.
23. UK foundational set: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yell,
    Thomson Local, FreeIndex, Facebook, plus the trade registries in §5. Identical NAP everywhere.
24. One canonical NAP string per client, stored in the studio's client record: exact name, exact
    address format (even though hidden on GBP), one phone number (prefer a local geographic or
    consistent mobile number — never mix numbers across listings), one canonical domain URL.
25. Fix inconsistencies before building anything new. Conflicting entity data undermines Google's
    confidence in the business record; consistency is the whole point of the exercise.

## 5. The UK trust ecosystem — entities Google understands

26. Treat NICEIC/NAPIT and MCS registry listings as first-class citations. NICEIC certifies ~38,000
    businesses and runs a public "Find a Tradesperson" directory; MCS is the UKAS-accredited
    certification scheme for small-scale renewables (and MCS certification is what unlocks
    Smart Export Guarantee payments for the customer). Claim/complete these listings with the
    canonical NAP and website link at onboarding.
27. Name the accreditations in on-page copy and mark them up (see §7). "NICEIC Approved
    Contractor", "MCS Certified (MCS number)", "Part P registered" are trust entities Google can
    associate with the business and are E-E-A-T evidence for what is safety-critical (YMYL-adjacent) work.
28. Use Checkatrade deliberately: it is barnacle SEO — Checkatrade category pages rank for many
    "electrician [town]" queries, it vets with up to 12 checks, offers a £1,000 homeowner
    guarantee, and holds a 4.6/5 Trustpilot rating from 75k+ reviews. A profile there wins visibility
    the client's own site won't get in year one. But keep Google reviews the primary ask — reviews
    left on Checkatrade don't move the local pack.
29. Which? Trusted Traders (assessed endorsement scheme) and Trustpilot are secondary trust
    badges: valuable on-site as conversion evidence and as brand-SERP real estate. Show real
    widgets/scores, never static fake stars.
30. Display accreditation logos with links to the client's actual registry entry (NICEIC/MCS
    checker pages) so humans and crawlers can verify the claim.

## 6. Site architecture — service × location without becoming a doorway farm

31. Build the hub first: homepage (brand + primary town), one page per service ("EV charger
    installation", "Fuse board upgrades", "Solar panel installation", "Battery storage",
    "EICR / electrical testing"), each targeting "[service] [primary town]".
32. Add location pages only for towns the client genuinely and regularly serves, chosen by search
    demand and job history (Sterling Sky's selection guidance) — typically 5–15 for a one-van firm,
    not 50 villages.
33. Know Google's official line and stay on the right side of it: doorway abuse includes "multiple
    domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page"
    (Google Search Essentials spam policies). One domain per client, and every location page must
    work as a final destination — full contact path, real content, no funnelling.
34. Make each location page verifiably local: photos of actual jobs in that town, reviews from
    customers in that town, named areas/streets served, town-specific service notes (stone-built
    housing stock, listed-building consent, local DNO quirks for solar). Sterling Sky's testing
    shows even ~85% similar service-area pages can rank — but unique proof-of-work content is what
    keeps them safe under the doorway/scaled-content policies and what converts.
35. Never orphan location pages: link them from the footer or a "Areas we cover" hub, and
    cross-link service pages ↔ location pages. Hidden pages are both a spam signal and a waste.
36. Do not spin one template across service × city combinatorially ("EV charger installation
    Halifax", "EV charger installation Brighouse"... × 8 services). Build service-in-town sections
    on the location page; only split out a dedicated service+town page when demand and unique
    content justify it.
37. Solar-specific: build for "solar panels [town]" and "solar panel installers [town]" as the
    head terms, with battery storage and EV charging as sibling services; lead with MCS status,
    real installs with generation figures, and finance/SEG explanations — content competitors' thin
    pages lack.

## 7. Schema — precise, singular, consistent

38. Use the most specific type that exists: `@type: "Electrician"` (a LocalBusiness subtype) for
    electricians. There is no solar-specific schema.org type — use `Electrician` (when the firm is
    an electrical contractor) or `LocalBusiness`/`HomeAndConstructionBusiness`, and name solar in
    `description`, `knowsAbout` and Service markup instead.
39. One LocalBusiness/Electrician block per site (on the homepage or every page via a single
    template) — never multiple contradictory blocks with mismatched names, phones, or hours.
    NAP in schema must match the GBP character-for-character.
40. Include: `name`, `url`, `telephone`, `address` (fine to include even when hidden on GBP — it's
    the registered business address), `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `image`, `priceRange`,
    `areaServed` (list the real towns; `areaServed` supersedes the older `serviceArea` property),
    and `sameAs` pointing to the GBP/Maps URL, Checkatrade, Trustpilot, Which? Trusted Traders,
    Facebook/Instagram, and the NICEIC/MCS registry entries.
41. Express accreditations in machine-readable form: `memberOf` (NICEIC, NAPIT) and
    `hasCredential` (MCS certification) on the Organization.
42. Mark up each service page with `Service` schema (`serviceType`, `provider`, `areaServed`) and
    FAQ content where genuine FAQs exist.
43. Keep expectations honest with clients: structured data supports organic rich results and entity
    understanding — the local pack itself is fed by GBP data, not by your JSON-LD. Schema is
    consistency infrastructure, not a pack-ranking switch.

## 8. On-page, links, prominence

44. Title tags: `[Service] in [Town] | [Brand]` on service/location pages; homepage
    `[Trade] in [Town] | [Brand]`. H1 matches search language ("Electrician in Harrogate"), not
    trade jargon.
45. Put the canonical NAP, accreditation logos, and review score in the sitewide footer; put
    phone number click-to-call in the header on mobile. Local pack clicks often bypass the site —
    the site's job for pack traffic is instant contact.
46. Publish proof-of-work continuously: job write-ups with photos, town-tagged. This is
    simultaneously location-page content, E-E-A-T evidence, and post material for GBP.
47. Earn a small number of real local links — the top local-organic factor group is inbound link
    quality/authority (Whitespark). Realistic sources for a trade: supplier/manufacturer installer
    directories (e.g. inverter/battery brands' "approved installer" pages), local sports club
    sponsorships, chamber of trade, local press for notable installs, wholesaler case studies.
48. One domain, forever. No exact-match microsites per town — that is the literal doorway example
    in Google's spam policy, and it splits the review/link equity that drives prominence.

## 9. Measurement

49. Track pack and organic separately: grid-based rank tracking for the pack (positions vary
    street-by-street with proximity), standard rank tracking for organic, GBP performance
    (calls, direction requests, website clicks) monthly, and UTM-tagged GBP sessions in analytics.
50. Report what the client feels: calls and form fills first, rankings second. Review count,
    rating, and recency go in every monthly report — they are both a ranking input and the
    conversion layer.

## The launch checklist

Ordered actions for a brand-new client site at go-live.

### Studio does (on-site / technical)
1. Confirm canonical NAP with client in writing; store in client record.
2. Build hub architecture: homepage + service pages + justified location pages (§6), all
   interlinked, none orphaned; titles/H1s per rule 44.
3. Footer: NAP, accreditation logos linked to registry entries, review score; header click-to-call.
4. JSON-LD: single Electrician/LocalBusiness block (rule 40–41), Service schema on service pages,
   validated in Google's Rich Results Test.
5. Location pages populated with real job photos, town-specific copy, and local testimonials
   supplied by the client — do not launch placeholder city pages.
6. Accreditation section: NICEIC/NAPIT, MCS (for solar), Part P, insurance — named in copy and schema.
7. XML sitemap + Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools submitted; analytics live; GBP website
   link UTM scheme defined.
8. Foundational citations built/corrected to canonical NAP: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect,
   Yell, Thomson Local, FreeIndex, Facebook.
9. Review display: genuine review widget/feed on site (DMCC publisher duty — real, dated,
   attributable reviews only).
10. Grid rank tracking + call tracking configured; baseline recorded.

### Client must do (GBP, reviews, registries) — studio guides, client executes
11. Create/claim Google Business Profile with real business name; enter address, then hide it
    (SAB); set service areas (≤ ~2h drive from base).
12. Complete video verification: continuous 30s+ live video showing local street signage plus
    branded van, tools, and materials.
13. Set primary category (Electrician / Solar energy company) and agreed secondaries; complete
    services, attributes, hours, description; upload 10+ real job photos.
14. Claim/complete NICEIC (or NAPIT) "Find a Tradesperson" and MCS registry listings with
    canonical NAP and website URL; align Checkatrade / Which? Trusted Traders / Trustpilot
    profiles if held.
15. Start the review engine: direct Google review link on a QR card and post-job SMS/email
    template; ask every satisfied customer; target steady monthly velocity, not a launch blitz.
16. No incentives, no gating, no fakes — signed DMCC/Google-policy acknowledgement in the
    onboarding pack (fines up to 10% of turnover / £300k).
17. Commit to a weekly rhythm: respond to every review within a week, one GBP post or photo
    upload per week or two, forward job photos per town to the studio monthly.
